How the projection works

Before retirement, income funds tax-advantaged contributions and estimated spending; the rest goes to taxable brokerage. A negative remainder draws from taxable investments. At retirement, income and new contributions stop. The withdrawal percentage applies to each year's opening retirement balance. Withdrawals cover spending first; excess goes to taxable brokerage, which also covers spending beyond the chosen withdrawal.

Both balances grow at the chosen rate. Growth applies to opening balances, then cash flows happen at year end. Withdrawals are capped at the retirement funds available after growth. Spending and working income rise with inflation. A funding gap means the chosen withdrawal plus taxable funds cannot cover spending; it does not necessarily mean retirement funds are exhausted. Gaps are shown separately, not carried forward as debt.

Tax-advantaged saving is a contribution allocation, not a calculated tax deduction. Contribution limits, taxes, withdrawal penalties, benefits, and market volatility are not modeled. These are illustrations, not predictions or a probability of success. Leaving or reloading clears your inputs and comparison.
