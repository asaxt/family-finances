How the projection works

Each person's income and retirement saving stop at their retirement age. Withdrawals use that person's opening pre-tax and Roth balances. Pre-tax distributions enter taxable income; qualified Roth distributions do not. Surplus after modeled taxes and shared spending goes to brokerage. A deficit draws from brokerage, then becomes a funding gap; it does not force extra retirement withdrawals or carry debt forward.

Growth applies to opening balances and cash flows occur at year end. Each projection period is a full year; the first year is not reduced for the current month. Both spouses are assumed alive throughout. These are illustrations with fixed rates, not predictions or a probability of success.

Tax coverage and assumptions

Uses 2026 federal ordinary-income brackets, standard and age deductions, Social Security, Medicare, and Additional Medicare. Oregon uses resident income and nonresident work income, standard deductions, a federal-tax subtraction, and personal exemption credits. Cross-state credits use a proportional planning estimate of tax on income taxed by both states.

Washington’s enacted 9.9% ordinary-income tax begins in 2028 above the shared married deduction of $1 million. Separate returns allocate that deduction equally. Future indexed amounts use the inflation assumption; Washington deduction indexing is modeled every two years beginning with 2029 income. Tax law can change.

Does not model investment capital gains/dividends, itemized deductions, dependents, most credits, AMT, self-employment, Social Security benefits, minimum distributions, withdrawal penalties, state paid-leave premiums, WA Cares, Oregon transit tax, or city/county taxes. Mixed community/separate ownership and part-year moves are not modeled. Use the projection as a planning estimate, not a tax return.
